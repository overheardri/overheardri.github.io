Open about how we work

From a meeting broadcast to a summary

We want anyone to know what was discussed in Indonesia's parliament without sitting through hours of meetings. That means we also have to be open about how these summaries are made, and where they can go wrong.

Five steps

Take the official broadcast

We only use open meetings streamed on the official channels of the DPR (including TVR Parlemen) and the Presidential Secretariat. We never re-record or edit the video; every page links to the original.

Turn speech into text

We transcribe the audio ourselves with a speech-recognition model (Whisper), keeping the timestamps. A few earlier meetings still use YouTube's automatic subtitles; every page names its source.

Split the meeting into parts

Long meetings are cut into parts of about 10 minutes. Every line keeps its original second, so every point can be traced back to the video.

Summarize with AI, neutrally

An AI model reads each part, then writes the overall summary. Early drafts can come from a small model on our own computer (Gemma), but published summaries use a stronger model (Claude, made by Anthropic), because in tests the small model misread jokes and numbers. The model is always named on the page. The rules: only report what was said, never judge anyone, never guess a party or a name, and skip jokes and small talk. The English edition is translated from the Indonesian by the same model, with no facts added.

Checked by a human

Until it's checked, every page is labeled AI draft, not yet checked. A reviewer matches points, names and numbers against the video, and the page is then labeled Checked by a human.

Limits you should know

  • NumbersAutomatic transcripts often mishear numbers (for example "Rp3.2 million" heard as "Rp3,200"). We always tag numbers check video.
  • NamesNames of people and institutions can be misspelled. We only attribute an opinion when the name or institution is clearly stated.
  • ContextJokes, sarcasm and interruptions can be misunderstood by a machine. For anything important, watch that part of the video.
  • CoverageWe don't cover every meeting yet. Closed meetings aren't public and we don't cover them.
  • TranslationThe English edition is a machine translation of the Indonesian summaries. Full transcripts stay in Indonesian, as spoken.

Our principles

  • NeutralWe don't take the side of any party, the government or the opposition. We report; we don't judge.
  • CheckableEvery point has its minute in the source video.
  • Open about AIEvery page names the model used and whether a human has checked it.
  • CorrectableFound a mistake? Report it, and we'll fix it and note the correction.

Legal basis

Under Article 42 of Indonesia's Copyright Law (Law No. 28 of 2014), there is no copyright in the proceedings of open meetings of state institutions. We still link every summary to the official broadcast as its source.
